D10 → D11 · THE HEAT PATH

Sources and model

This original schematic follows one liquid-cooled heat path: chip → cold plate → technology coolant → liquid-to-liquid CDU → facility water → outdoor dry cooler. Equipment position and geometry are illustrative. CDUs can be in a rack or beside a row; this drawing places one beside the racks.

What the numerical example means

The selected liquid path receives a hypothetical 100 kW of heat at steady state. Water is modeled with constant specific heat of 4.18 kJ/(kg·K). At 5 kg/s, its temperature rise is 4.78 K; at 2.5 kg/s, it is 9.57 K. These are temperature differences, not coolant inlet temperatures or chip temperatures.

We omit pump heat and heat exchanged with room air. The 100 kW is heat already captured by this liquid path; it does not imply a 100 kW rack has no residual air-cooling load. The arithmetic alone does not establish pump performance, exchanger capacity or acceptable device temperatures.

What a flow interruption tells us

Stopping facility circulation removes the normal steady transport path from the CDU to outdoor rejection. Flow and stored energy inside the technology loop can persist. Fluid, equipment and exchanger metal can temporarily store heat; no time-to-overheat or immediate zero heat transfer follows from this drawing. Restoring flow reconnects the route, without predicting a recovery time.

Why this particular architecture

The two fluids stay separated by the CDU heat exchanger in normal operation. The cold plate contacts the processor package through a thermal interface; coolant is inside the cold plate, not on the silicon junction. A dry cooler transfers heat from facility fluid through a coil into outdoor air. Its operating envelope depends on ambient and coolant temperatures. Other designs use refrigeration, evaporative rejection or additional loops.
