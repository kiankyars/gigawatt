Inside the facility

First teaching section · selected D01 foundations. Eight scenes introduce paths, space, meter boundaries, heat, power versus energy, measurement resolution and useful work. The following workload section is still to be authored. All quantities here are hypothetical; diagrams show functional relationships, not installation drawings.

Model assumptions and equations

One facility ledger: ten racks at 100 kW each plus 100 kW of separately metered network and storage equipment make 1,100 kW of IT input. Upstream electrical losses (40 kW), cooling machinery (160 kW) and other facility loads (20 kW) add 220 kW. Facility input is 1,320 kW. The 8 kW of internal supply loss in each rack is already part of its 100 kW input. The rack boundary is an equipment-input account; moving a separately powered CDU into a rack does not automatically classify its power as IT energy.

Heat: over a steady interval, approximately all this electrical input ultimately leaves as heat. This assumes negligible net stored-energy change and small other energy streams. Information describes the service; it is not a large energy output to subtract from the heat balance. The heat arrows do not imply all 1,320 kW passes through one coolant loop.

Power and energy: 1 W = 1 J/s; 1 MW = 1,000 kW. Energy = power × duration for a constant segment. Add segment energies for a stepped profile. Average power = total energy ÷ elapsed time. The 24-hour traces both use 184 MWh; shifting flexible work is assumed to preserve that energy and its completion requirements. The illustrative 8 MW supply limit concerns the plotted demand only; real equipment has additional constraints.

Measurement resolution: 8 MW for five minutes and 12 MW for one minute followed by 7 MW for four minutes each use 666.7 kWh. Both average 8 MW, but their plotted peaks differ. These assumed piecewise-constant traces cannot establish a real device's millisecond response or protective-trip behavior.

Useful work: a separate one-hour comparison holds 100 identical completed jobs and their quality requirements fixed. Case A uses 1,000 kWh IT + 200 kWh overhead. Case B uses 1,200 kWh IT + 200 kWh overhead. PUE = facility energy ÷ IT energy. The ratio falls from 1.20 to about 1.17, while facility energy per job rises from 12 to 14 kWh. Fixed overhead is an assumption, not a general response to a changing IT load. This is not an annual measured PUE or a hardware benchmark.

Primary sources and reading limits · checked 11 September 2026
  • DOE / NREL — Best Practices Guide for Energy-Efficient Data Center Design: executive summary and sections 2.2, 2.4, 2.5, 7.1 and 8.1 support system boundaries, internal server consumption and heat recovery. Historical equipment-efficiency figures are not used in these examples.
  • EIA — Measuring electricity: power and energy units. The traces and supply limits are original examples.
  • Leviton — White Space and Gray Space: area definitions only; no universal equipment placement or connector configuration is inferred.
  • NVIDIA — NVL72 system hardware and components: component hierarchy and electrical, thermal and network interfaces. This diagram is generic and does not reproduce an NVL72 installation or use its product ratings.
  • The Green Grid — PUE: A Comprehensive Examination of the Metric: printed pages 8–9, 14–22 and 34 distinguish infrastructure efficiency, IT energy and productivity. Used as metric explanation, not a claim of compliance with a current standard.
