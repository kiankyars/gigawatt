# ERCOT large-load queue: evidence checked 20 September 2026

Teaching claim: **a request for power is an option to develop a project, not electricity consumption or a promise that the grid will serve it.** ERCOT's mid-2026 tracked requests were approaching half a terawatt. Much of the pipeline had not completed studies. That supports skepticism about treating the entire pipeline as future demand, but it does not establish what percentage is fictitious, duplicated, or ultimately cancelled.

## 1. Use a dated primary snapshot

[ERCOT, Senate Business & Commerce update, 29 July 2026, slide 6](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/07/29/ERCOT-Senate-July-29-Panel-1-Assessing-The-Grid.pdf#page=6) reports **June 2026** large-load data. The cumulative 2033 column is **474.7 GW**:

| Status in that snapshot | GW |
| --- | ---: |
| No studies submitted | 284.3 |
| Under ERCOT review | 135.5 |
| Section 9.4 requirements only | 9.9 |
| Section 9.4/9.5 requirements met | 36.0 |
| Approved to energize, not operational | 3.2 |
| Observed energized | 5.9 |

Rounded components differ from the displayed total by 0.1 GW. The deck identifies approximately 90% of requested large-load MW as data centers. Its separate generation queue, slide 7, contains **462,785 MW and 1,949 requests as of 30 June 2026**. Those are proposed supply/storage projects, not load requests.

**Chart handling:** label the total “tracked large-load pipeline through 2033, June 2026 snapshot.” The future-year bars are cumulative requested ramps; do not add them. The small operational segment beside future requests is a maturity comparison, not a measured project failure rate. Approximately 88% is in the first two status categories (calculation from displayed values).

## 2. What the categories actually prove

[ERCOT March 2026 operational overview, published 16 April 2026](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/04/16/ERCOT-Monthly-Operational-Overview-March-2026.pdf) defines observed energized as operational projects represented by their **all-time, non-simultaneous peak consumption**. It is not simultaneous ERCOT system demand and is not a current IT-load measurement. Approved-to-energize projects have operations approval but are not yet observed operational. “No studies submitted” includes projects lacking sufficient information for review **and** MW not approved after planning review until a viable interconnection path appears or the request is cancelled. Consequently, that category is not a direct count of fake applications.

The July 2026 deck adds the distinction between Section 9.4-only and Section 9.4/9.5-complete status. Preserve its actual labels if reproducing the current chart; do not collapse every completed engineering step into “power approved.”

## 3. Batch Zero gives a second, different filter

[ERCOT House State Affairs update, 19 August 2026, slide 3](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/08/19/ERCOTPanel1DataCenters.pdf#page=3) republishes **28 July 2026** preliminary eligibility:

| Preliminary Batch Zero category | GW, rounded | Projects |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Eligible as base load | 65 | 150 |
| Eligible, base/allocated classification unresolved | 25 | 49 |
| Eligible as allocated load | 114 | 127 |
| Excluded: no dynamic model | 20 | 47 |
| Excluded: no qualifying study | 274 | 315 |

ERCOT calls the eligible total approximately **205 GW**; rounded bars sum to 204. This is eligibility to enter a study process, not 205 GW approved to operate. The universe and date differ from the June pipeline. Do not force the two charts into one conserved numerical funnel or call the excluded amounts cancellations. The August deck changed the earlier expected classification date to later in the year.

## 4. Speculation and duplicate requests: evidence, not a fabricated percentage

[ERCOT's 3 February 2026 Batch Study workshop transcript](https://ercot.new.swagit.com/videos/373914) records stakeholder concerns about speculative load and says the barrier to entry could be low depending on location. Search-indexed primary transcript wording supports the historical incentive explanation; the transcript page itself blocked direct retrieval during this research. No verified universal historical “zero dollar” entry price was found.

[SB 6 enrolled text, PURA §37.0561(d)](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/SB00006F.htm) requires disclosure of substantially similar Texas service requests whose approval would materially change, delay, or withdraw another request. This is direct evidence that alternative requests matter to planning; it does **not** quantify duplicate MW. The same law directs a study fee of at least **$100,000** for initial transmission screening, additional fees for added capacity, crediting unused initial study fees toward eligible same-site obligations, site-control requirements, and financial commitments for necessary transmission infrastructure. Security can be refunded after outstanding obligations and specified milestones. See subsections (f)–(i).

Safe wording: “Relatively inexpensive early requests can preserve several development options. ERCOT has to distinguish those options from committed, study-ready projects. Texas is adding study fees, security, site-control evidence, and disclosure of overlapping requests.” Mark this incentive mechanism as interpretation; no source reviewed establishes that most requests are fraudulent or that a specific percentage will never be built.

## 5. Current commitments make ‘free to join’ misleading

[PGRR145 official status](https://www.ercot.com/mktrules/issues/PGRR145): approved **18 June 2026**, principally effective **11 July 2026**. [Final PUCT report DOCX](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/06/22/145PGRR-130-PUCT-Report-061826.docx), Section 9.2.1.2(1)(c), requires the studied/allocated eligibility path to provide **$50,000 per MW of peak demand in the latest Load Commissioning Plan** as financial security. Acceptable forms include cash, qualifying corporate/parent guarantees, and qualifying bank letters of credit. Therefore $50 million on a 1 GW request is the **face amount of security**, not necessarily a cash expenditure or nonrefundable fee. Some base-load paths use actual upgrade cost allocation; specific no-upgrade circumstances allow zero system-upgrade security. Those exceptions do not mean every project interconnects for free or avoids other costs.

Illustrative arithmetic at that security rate:

| Peak demand | Security face amount |
| --- | ---: |
| 100 MW | $5 million |
| 500 MW | $25 million |
| 1,000 MW | $50 million |

**Final rule independently verified in a follow-up:** [PUCT docket 58481, item 218](https://interchange.puc.texas.gov/search/documents/?controlNumber=58481&itemNumber=218) adopts §25.194 on **18 September 2026**. Its [official Texas Register acknowledgment, item 219](https://interchange.puc.texas.gov/Documents/58481_219_1684678.PDF#page=3), records **8 October 2026** as the effective date. It is adopted but not yet effective as of this memo.

- [Printed pages 234–235 / PDF pages 35–36](https://interchange.puc.texas.gov/Documents/58481_218_1684656.PDF#page=35), §25.194(d)(2): **$100,000 study fee**, due by intermediate-agreement execution; unused amount returned within 60 days after the study. ERCOT protocols may impose an additional flat study fee. Intermediate security is **$50,000/MW** of requested peak demand for a new request, or incremental peak increase for an expansion.
- [Printed pages 251–252 / PDF pages 52–53](https://interchange.puc.texas.gov/Documents/58481_218_1684656.PDF#page=52), §25.194(f)(2)(B): security at Standard Large Load Interconnection Agreement (SLLIA) execution is the **greater of $50,000/MW contracted peak demand or allocated system-upgrade costs**. Additional security related to minimum billing demand may apply.
- [Printed page 217 / PDF page 18](https://interchange.puc.texas.gov/Documents/58481_218_1684656.PDF#page=18) confirms elimination of the proposed interconnection fee. Security remains distinct from fees and direct construction contributions. Withdrawal/return conditions vary; avoid presenting security as unconditionally refundable.

## 6. As-of-date status: verification is still underway

[ERCOT 20 August 2026 PUCT presentation, slides 2 and 7](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/08/20/Batch-Zero-Verification-and-Audit-PUCT-Presentation.pdf) says ERCOT paused approvals to energize data centers/crypto facilities **75 MW or greater** pending Batch Zero verification. It distinguishes smaller medium loads and non-data-center large loads from that pause. Its evidence requirements include site documents, long-lead equipment orders, end-user contracts, contractor agreements, utility confirmations of financial security, and disclosures about substantially similar requests. The audit is designed to test the evidence behind eligibility assertions.

[ERCOT notice 9 September 2026](https://www.ercot.com/services/comm/mkt_notices/M-A090926-01) confirms verification RFIs began for conditionally included loads, with additional requests continuing through September. Complete documentation and notarized attestations generally are due within ten business days, with a possible five-business-day extension. Passing verification is required for inclusion; failure can exclude a load. Therefore, as of **20 September**, do not say that the audit is complete, that a conditional classification guarantees energization, or that a final cleaned queue has been published.

[ERCOT June 1–2 board system operations update, slides 3–4](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/05/24/7-System-Operations-Update.pdf) distinguishes raw submissions from the forecast methodology: ERCOT proposed an adjustment based on verified historical realization or on Batch Zero base loads plus the economic forecast and medium loads, and preferred the latter. The [20 August update, slide 4](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/08/20/Batch-Zero-Verification-and-Audit-PUCT-Presentation.pdf#page=4) explains that verification delays the final base-load classification and thus the long-term forecast. A queue statistic is not automatically ERCOT's accepted system peak-demand forecast.

## 7. Three teachable visual cases

1. **Read the status before reading the MW.** Recreate the June snapshot's six maturity segments, show source date and through-year, and let learners expand what each stage proves. Put “operational consumption,” “engineering progress,” and “requested future demand” in separate explanations. Source art: July 29 PDF slide 6; readily downloadable and visually inspected.
2. **One tenant, several possible sites.** Explicitly hypothetical: one 1 GW deployment explores three alternative utility locations. The requests add to 3 GW; the mutually exclusive business plan adds at most 1 GW if built. Then reveal evidence cards for site control, tenant contract, studies, and financial security. Tie disclosure to SB 6, without pretending this example estimates ERCOT's duplicate share. Show security arithmetic as an obligation scale, separately from the initial study fee and total construction cost.
3. **A study-entry gate is not the switch-on gate.** Use the August 19 slide 3 Batch Zero categories, then a separate sequence: conditional classification → document verification → applicable engineering/stability work → approval to energize → observed operation. Include a September 9 date chip showing verification underway. This explains why a press release saying “included in Batch Zero” is not evidence of operating MW. Keep exact engineering routes qualified because base and studied loads have different paths.

## 8. Figure C cross-check: staged energization

The [4 May 2026 ERCOT workshop page](https://www.ercot.com/calendar/05042026-Batch-Study-Process-for) links the [original PPTX](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/05/04/ERCOT_Batch_Study_Workshop_8_20260504.pptx). Slide **38** was independently extracted and explicitly says **ILLUSTRATIVE**. It depicts a 1,000 MW load, a 100 MW withdrawal limit, and two 500 MW generators arriving in stages. The slide requires load to remain within available generation plus permitted withdrawal, including contingencies, subject to stability limits. Its final “≤1,100” arithmetic is a supply-side ceiling; the pictured site's requested load remains 1,000 MW. The course should cap its own example at 1,000 MW.

This is safe as a **mechanism illustration with a May 2026 date**, not a real site, guaranteed schedule, or permission for a particular load to energize. The underlying Withdrawal-Limited Private Use Network (WLPUN) pathway did reach adoption: the [26 June ERCOT implementation notice](https://www.ercot.com/services/comm/mkt_notices/M-B062326-02) applies PGRR145 and specifies the generator-study deadline for WLPUN entrants. The final PGRR145 report's §9.3.2.2 caps allocated load at the lowest of requested demand, withdrawal limit plus generation rating, and its stability-derived cap. Section 9.4.2 requires the corresponding agreement and commissioning plan. Preserve those gates; onsite generation alone does not grant an interconnection entitlement.

## Editorial guardrails

- “About half a terawatt of tracked requests in mid-2026” is supported. “ERCOT now consumes/requires 474.7 GW” is false.
- No verified exact September-wide total was located. The best fully inspected status breakdown is dated June and published July 29; publish that date prominently.
- Do not call the entire large-load queue AI: it includes crypto, industry, hydrogen, and unspecified projects. The data-center classification itself does not prove every MW is AI training.
- Do not compare future requested peaks to today's operating MW and label the difference a cancellation rate.
- Generation nameplate MW and battery discharge MW do not equal dependable capacity available to serve new load; keep the supply queue out of a demand total.
- Keep money labels precise: study fee, financial security face amount, construction contribution, and actual project capital cost are different quantities.
