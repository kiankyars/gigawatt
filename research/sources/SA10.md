## Research takeaway — 2026-09-06

The accessible material presents several conversion placements and an adoption
sequence. It is useful for constructing a comparison, not for declaring one
universal 800 V DC topology. Map its discussion across building distribution,
rack conversion, storage/protection and retrofit constraints.

## Cross-check and teaching decision

Compare [P05](P05.md), the earlier NVIDIA roadmap, with [P16](P16.md), the
August 2026 announcement. Keep dates attached. For D06.3 and capstone C03, draw
the architectures in a shared frame and ask which upstream AC constraints
remain when a near-rack converter is introduced.

## Still to verify

Exact architecture specifications, protection interfaces, efficiency curves,
retrofit constraints and deployment status. Savings and timing claims require
their stated baseline and assumptions; these notes do not review the full paid
report or underlying models.

## 2026-09-11: checked adoption dates

The May 26, 2026 article forecasts Phase 1 in 2026/2027, Phase 2 in
2027/2028, and Phase 3 in late 2028/2029. The first two share an AC-fed
power rack; the 800 V step-down moves from a rack shelf to the blade.
Phase 3 places low-voltage rectification upstream after conventional step-down.
The article also discusses distributed DC-coupled backup replacing central UPS
functions, while expecting some operators to retain central UPS equipment. The
placement drawings omit backup; their shared sidecar does not imply identical
rack internals or a universal backup topology.

The sample's three architecture drawings are the conventional AC baseline,
grouped Phases 1–2, and Phase 3. They are not three consecutive numbered
SemiAnalysis phases. Phase 4 is the MV/SST proposal: its heading says >2029,
while its body says early 2029. Preserve that discrepancy rather than selecting
one date. None of these dates establishes an installed configuration.
