## Reviewed teaching claims

DC current keeps its direction; the steady example also keeps its magnitude.
In a sinusoidal AC resistor circuit, voltage and current reverse together, so
instantaneous load power stays nonnegative. The waveform's RMS value expresses
its heating equivalent in a resistor; it differs from both peak and signed mean.

## Original worked comparison

Hold receiving-end average power at 100 kW. With 480 V RMS across a single
resistor, current is 208.33 A RMS and instantaneous power ranges from zero to
200 kW. Its average is 100 kW: over one hour it receives 100 kWh. An 800 V steady
DC case needs 125 A and delivers 100 kW continuously. Different load resistances
make these equal-power examples; this is not one unchanged resistor switched
between the supplies. Conductor losses and conversion are introduced later.
