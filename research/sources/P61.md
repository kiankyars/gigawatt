## Claim-level notes

Reviewed 2026-09-11. Printed page 8 separates PUE from productivity;
pages 14–20 locate measurements and classify equipment; pages 21–22 and 34
discuss the time basis. In-rack cooling remains facility equipment. White/gray
space labels therefore cannot determine the PUE category. IT-input measurements
must exclude non-IT loads.

## Teaching use

The D01 ending uses an original counterexample: **a lower PUE can accompany more
energy for the same useful work**. Assume an all-electric facility and two steady
one-hour operating cases. Each completes 100 identical useful jobs, with the same
quality and service requirements. IT power is measured at the same equipment-input
boundary in both cases. Facility overhead is a separate account, assumed constant
at 200 kW for this comparison; that is not a general cooling-load relationship.

| Quantity | Case A | Case B |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| IT power | 1,000 kW | 1,200 kW |
| Facility overhead | 200 kW | 200 kW |
| Total facility energy in one hour | 1,200 kWh | 1,400 kWh |
| IT energy in the same hour | 1,000 kWh | 1,200 kWh |
| Useful jobs completed | 100 | 100 |
| Illustrative interval PUE | 1.20 | 1.167 |
| Facility energy per useful job | 12 kWh | 14 kWh |

Calculate each ratio from energy over the same hour. PUE falls from
1,200 / 1,000 to 1,400 / 1,200 while facility energy per job rises by one-sixth.
The numerical cases are teaching assumptions, not operating measurements or a
claim about annual PUE. The result does not make PUE useless: it demonstrates
why the useful-work account must remain separate from the facility/IT ratio.

For D15, retain the same accounting distinction when assessing operating changes.
A favorable ratio alone does not establish an improvement in energy per completed
job; the workload, measurement boundary and elapsed interval must stay explicit.
