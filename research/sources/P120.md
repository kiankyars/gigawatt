## Claim-level notes

Reviewed 2026-09-12: Microsoft, Scott Guthrie, November 12, 2025. The section “High-availability, low-cost power” describes Fairwater Atlanta as capable of four-nines availability at three-nines cost because its utility supply is highly available.

For the GPU fleet, Microsoft says this supports omitting traditional on-site generation, UPS and dual-corded distribution. The next paragraph separately describes on-site energy storage for smoothing power fluctuations. The correct scope is the GPU fleet's traditional backup architecture, not “no batteries anywhere at the campus.”

This is an operator **design/capability claim**. The announcement supplies no measured annual outage record, facility SLA, Uptime Tier certification or quantified capital-cost comparator. Four nines and three nines are availability/cost shorthand here, not Tier IV and Tier III.

## Teaching use

Use the named Fairwater site to explain how utility reliability and the GPU service influence the investment in local backup. All four Uptime Tiers include generation, but an operator does not have to choose an Uptime Tier architecture. This distinction prevents the generator-baseline slide and this case from sounding contradictory.

The actual Microsoft aerial is stored unchanged at `course/assets/references/microsoft-fairwater-atlanta.jpg`; source URL and SHA-256 are in the reference provenance file. The photo establishes visual context, not the reliability claim.

## Unestablished claims

Do not infer that checkpointing, workload migration or a specific recovery time is implemented from Microsoft's power-design section.
