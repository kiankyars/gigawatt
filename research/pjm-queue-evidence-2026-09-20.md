# PJM large-load connection evidence — checked 2026-09-20

Primary-source research for Chapter 17. Historical observations, forecasts, requests, contract capacity and rule proposals must retain their dates and labels. No future milestone below is asserted to have occurred.

## Core distinction: there is more than one connection process

**Teach:** a generator queue measures proposed supply connections; it is not a list of data centers waiting for power. Avoid the stronger, incorrect claim that load can never appear in PJM's New Services Queue.

[FERC, June 18, 2026, EL26-67-000, printed pp. 19–21](https://www.ferc.gov/sites/default/files/2026-06/EL26-67-000.pdf) describes two load paths: an eligible customer requests new transmission service, or an existing network customer designates additional network load and modifies its agreement. PJM's New Services Queue includes generation, merchant transmission **and long-term firm transmission-service requests**. Study results estimate facilities, cost responsibility and construction lead times. This order's pp. 30–44 seek clearer large-load processes, including 60–90-day studies: that is neither an existing blanket energization guarantee nor a 60–90-day construction promise. It is a show-cause proceeding, with preliminary findings and directions to explain or propose tariff revisions. Federal transmission rules and state retail responsibilities both matter.

[PJM generator-cycle announcement, August 3, 2026](https://insidelines.pjm.com/over-700-new-generation-projects-accepted-into-first-cycle-of-reformed-interconnection-process/): 715 generation projects, 201.5 GW of **nameplate** capacity, qualified for Cycle 1 study. PJM describes a designed study duration of one to two years. Applications are not built resources; nameplate is not accredited dependable capacity. The article also identifies permitting and supply-chain obstacles after signed agreements. Use this only as the supply-side comparison, not a data-center connection queue or guaranteed commissioning schedule.

## Best concrete case: Dominion's commitment ladder

[Dominion letter to PJM, January 6, 2026, pp. 1–3](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/planning/res-adeq/load-forecast/dominion-documentation.pdf):

| Stage | Meaning in this utility's account |
| --- | --- |
| ELOA: engineering authorization | Site control and engineered site plan precede a $250,000 engineering deposit. Typical study: 9–12 months. Outputs: infrastructure, estimated energization date, estimated cost. Customer may decline to proceed; classified non-firm. |
| CLOA: construction authorization | Capacity reserved; customer bears cancellation costs, commits to an ESA and funds long-lead equipment deposits. Classified firm for forecasting. |
| ESA: electric service agreement | Required before setting the meter/service; specifies contracted capacity, collateral and applicable tariff. |

The July 2025 contract snapshot totals **47 GW: 9.8 ESA + 7.1 CLOA + 30.1 ELOA**. Dominion reports **4 GW coincident data-center peak in 2025** and forecasts **16.6 GW demand in 2046**. These are different measures and dates, not an attrition funnel. Scope: Dominion Energy's customer portfolio, not the whole PJM region or all Northern Virginia utilities. The deposit buys engineering work, not a universal PJM power reservation. A forecast's “firm” category does not establish a guaranteed date or absence of curtailment rights.

## Regional forecast: commitment screens change the planning number

[PJM January 2026 Load Forecast, pp. 4–6](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/library/reports-notices/load-forecast/2026-load-report.pdf): near-term large-load adjustments require stronger commitments, such as service obligations/construction commitments; longer-term non-firm projects are derated. The 2026 forecast is lower than the 2025 forecast through 2032; the change reflects large-load adjustments, electric vehicles and economics, so do not attribute all of it to data-center cancellations. Forecast summer peak for 2036: 222,106 MW, 65,733 MW above 2026. This is total regional forecast load, not a requested-MW queue.

[PJM 2026 forecast supplement, pp. 33–37](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/planning/res-adeq/load-forecast/load-forecast-supplement-2026.pdf) explains the Dominion-zone treatment, including Dominion, NOVEC, ODEC and REC data. Historical data-center load is removed from model-estimation inputs before explicit additions to avoid double counting. Keep “Dominion zone” distinct from “Dominion Energy customers.”

## Optional historical co-location case: Susquehanna

[FERC concurrence, February 20, 2025](https://ferc.gov/news-events/news/commissioner-phillips-concurrence-statement-co-location-issues) records the earlier rejection of an ISA amendment increasing permitted co-located load from 300 to 480 MW. Teach this as the specific amendment dispute, not a ban on co-location.

[Talen's June 11, 2025 SEC exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1622536/000162828025030559/a20250611talenbusinessup.htm) announced an expanded PPA to 1,920 MW at full quantity and planned a transition to front-of-meter retail supply after grid connection, retaining 300 MW behind the meter in the interim. This is an announcement and contract structure, not proof of current operating MW or of completed reconfiguration. The lesson: a power-purchase agreement and physical transmission/service rights are different instruments. Prefer the Dominion case if slide space is limited.

## ERCOT comparison without a misleading speed ranking

| Question | ERCOT | PJM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is being studied? | A documented large-load integration process, distinct from generation interconnection. | Utility service/construction processes plus applicable PJM transmission/planning processes; generator-cycle totals are supply proposals. |
| Concrete 2026 change | [June 18 ERCOT/PUCT approval](https://www.ercot.com/news/release/06182026-puct-approves-ercots): Batch Zero groups qualified requests of at least 75 MW for a common study instead of repetitive project-by-project evaluation. | FERC large-load show-cause proceeding; PJM proposed IRAS/registry and ride-through changes. Status and scope differ. |
| Final operating gate | [ERCOT Large Load Integration](https://www.ercot.com/services/rq/large-load-integration) requires approval of an energization request; study acceptance alone is not energization authorization. | Verify the relevant utility's completed facilities and service agreement, transmission prerequisites and applicable operating conditions for the particular site. |

For a common numerical lesson, [ERCOT January 2026 Monthly, January 21 snapshot](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/02/13/ERCOT-Monthly-January-2026-FINAL.pdf) reports approximately 232,500 MW tracked requests, 8,786 MW approved to energize and 3,977 MW observed non-simultaneous peak. These are dated status measures, not conversion probabilities or a current September queue total. Do not rank ERCOT versus PJM “years to power” using incomparable supply and load queues.

## Three simple visuals to implement

1. **Two process lanes:** generation developer → PJM supply studies → network upgrades/agreement → built supply; data-center customer → utility engineering/construction/service → applicable PJM transmission/planning coordination → energized load. Connect both lanes to the same network. Add the transmission-service exception described above.
2. **Commitment ladder:** use the three Dominion stages in the table, with outputs and obligations revealed progressively. Keep a requested date separate from the study's estimated date and the eventual authorized service milestone. Do not invent a common total duration.
3. **Status bars:** use the ERCOT January snapshot as three separately labeled bars, with date and non-simultaneous qualifier. Optionally show the Dominion contract mix in a separate panel. Never stack requested capacity, coincident forecast demand and observed load as if they were the same accounting series.

## Phrases to avoid

- “PJM's 200 GW queue is waiting data centers.” It is generator nameplate in the cited Cycle 1 announcement.
- “Firm forecast load has a firm connection date.” These are different claims.
- “A study deposit buys power.” It funds a defined process and must be read with its specific contract.
- “Co-location avoids all interconnection and transmission obligations.” Configuration and rights matter.
- “PJM requires all data centers to bring their own power.” The verified August source is a proposal with qualifications.
- “The grid can connect any requested MW once the paperwork is done.” Facilities, supply adequacy, operating behavior and service rights still matter.
