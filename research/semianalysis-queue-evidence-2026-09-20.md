# SemiAnalysis evidence for an interconnection mini chapter

Reviewed 2026-09-20. Scope: publicly visible article text and figures, inspected in an unsigned-in browser through each article's visible paywall boundary. No subscriber-only article body, model, dashboard, or data was accessed. Figures were visually inspected in the browser; no media was downloaded. Existing catalog records SA07, SA32, SA33 and SA34 were reviewed. The exact public image links below are ready for a separate authorized asset-import step.

## Recommended chapter story

Organize the mini chapter around **what a developer can actually claim at each stage**. A request describes desired service; a study identifies constraints; agreements allocate obligations; installed equipment and operational approval enable actual service. Keep the generator-interconnection process separate from the large-load connection process throughout.

Suggested sequence:

1. **What is waiting in which queue?** Put a generator and a datacenter on opposite sides of one network. Explain why adding a generator and adding a load require different studies and processes. A queue is a set of proposals with dependencies, rather than a reliable countdown to power.
2. **Why are requested megawatts so much larger than built projects?** Use Figure A as a dated analyst comparison. Ask which quantities are operator requests and which are the publisher's modeled buildout.
3. **What turns an application into a credible commitment?** Use current primary ERCOT/PJM sources for study charges, site control, deposits, financial security, withdrawal consequences, and upgrade obligations. A study fee, refundable security, network-upgrade payment, electricity charge, and capacity-market charge are different costs. Avoid applying one region's rules to the other.
4. **How does a planner turn requests into a forecast?** Figure B provides historical visual context. Apply current primary sources to explain the present process. Treat queue totals, adjusted forecasts, coincident peak load, and delivered electricity as distinct quantities.
5. **Why can a completed study still leave a project waiting?** Draw parallel dependencies for network upgrades, generation availability, equipment, permits, finance, and commissioning. The PJM primary-source research should establish the current status and numbers.
6. **Can part of the load connect sooner?** Figure C provides an illustrative staged-energization mechanism. Define withdrawal limits and onsite generation explicitly. End with a developer comparison: ready equipment, realistic ramp, contractual service, and exposure to curtailment matter alongside headline MW.

Prefer two article figures in the main flow (A and C) and B in an optional explanation, unless the chapter needs extra forecast detail. Original course drawings should carry the central concepts; dense reference figures work as evidence to inspect. Maintain separate dates for publication, underlying data, forecast horizon, and this review.

## Exclusions and useful follow-up

- The national map in **SA33** is a poor candidate: its headline uses GW, while its legend and labels use MW; Texas shows 230,000 MW while the later article graphic uses April 2026's 410 GW. The vintage mismatch is not explained in the visible caption. Do not use it as the current national queue snapshot.
- **SA34**, [$12B of US ratepayers' money wasted on a modeling mistake and PJM wants to do it again](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/12b-of-us-ratepayers-money-wasted), August 16, 2026, is a reading-list option. Its financial loss estimate and modeling criticism are contested analytical conclusions, not suitable as unqualified teaching facts. The repository's existing caution remains appropriate.
- The SemiAnalysis [Energy Model product page](https://semianalysis.com/energy-model/) mentions a July 9, 2026 interconnection research item. This pass did not establish an accessible article body or public diagram for that item; do not imply that it was reviewed.
- Existing repository media includes `semianalysis-datacenter-delays-user.png`, `semianalysis-btm-by-state-2026.png`, and electrical architecture/forecast references. The new figures above are distinct. Preserve article URL, publisher, original figure credit, publication date, data date, dimensions, and import date in any asset provenance record.

## Verification handoff

The primary-source agents should establish current ERCOT and PJM procedures and dates independently. In particular, verify whether the May 2026 ERCOT workshop proposals were adopted or changed, and avoid presenting generator queue reform as a large-load connection entitlement. This memo's recommendation is conceptual: make the learner distinguish a request, forecast, contracted service, and actual energized capacity, then use region-specific evidence to explain their transitions.
